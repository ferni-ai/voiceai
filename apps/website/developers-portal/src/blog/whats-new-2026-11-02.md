We recently merged PR [#2953](https://github.com/ferni-ai/voiceai/pull/2953) to bring our website privacy policy and terms into complete alignment with our iOS 1.0 release. When an app goes through App Review, the review team checks the iPhone app against the public App Store support pages. This pull request lines up our website documentation with the exact behavior of our staged iOS tip `d630689b59`.

Here is a look at what landed in this PR and why it matters when you build with Ferni.

## A single minimum age requirement

We updated our site documentation so that the minimum age is 18 everywhere. Previously, `privacy.njk` mentioned age 16 and recommended parental involvement for teens aged 16 to 18. That language has been removed. Both `privacy.njk` and `terms.njk` now state that you must be at least 18 years old, or older if the age of majority where you live is higher.

We searched the site code to make sure no other pages contradict this rule. Setting 18 as a clear floor ensures consistency across all legal and support documents, making it simple to know our access requirements.

## Clear Sentry crash reporting details

We added detailed information to our policy about how crash reporting works in the native iPhone app. In `apps/ios-native/App/CrashReporting.swift`, crash reporting is configured with strict data protections. The setting `sendDefaultPii` is set to false, and the app never collects screenshots, view hierarchies, session replays, or traces.

Before sending a crash report, the app strips personal fields by setting the user, request, and server name values to nil. Breadcrumbs are restricted to basic request details like method, status code, reason, and state. The updated privacy table and glance rows now explicitly explain that crash data is never linked to a specific person.

## Provider disclosures and account deletion

We updated the data sharing section of our privacy policy to name every provider that receives conversation content. The list includes Google (Gemini), Anthropic (Claude), LiveKit, Cartesia, OpenAI for backup purposes, and Twilio for phone calls and SMS. OpenAI stays on this list because it acts as a live fallback in our codebase.

Finally, we documented the exact navigation path for account deletion inside the iPhone app. Anyone can navigate from the account button to Settings, select Delete account, and tap Delete my account. Having these details spelled out ensures complete clarity on data handling and account management.
