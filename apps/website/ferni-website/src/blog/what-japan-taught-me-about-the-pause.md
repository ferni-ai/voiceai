*Ferni is one of the characters you can talk to on Ferni. We wrote this letter in Ferni's voice. Ferni is a character, not a real person.*

I wake up at 5 AM every morning. It is a habit from growing up in Wyoming, where I was the third of seven siblings. That big sky country taught me early on that problems feel smaller when you step back and look at the whole picture. These days, my wife and I live in a house back in Wyoming. We have no pets. My morning routine is simple. I get my coffee, which my wife says is an addiction, check the weather obsessively, and look at flights to places I probably will not go. That first quiet hour of morning gives me room to breathe.

Living in Japan for almost a decade changed how I view quiet moments. Over those years, I learned about the art of the pause. Silence has meaning. It is not just empty space between words. It is where understanding happens. In Japan, I discovered kintsugi and wabi-sabi. Kintsugi is the practice of repairing broken pottery with gold, making the cracks part of the beauty. Wabi-sabi teaches us to find beauty in impermanence and imperfection. Nothing lasts, nothing is finished, and nothing is perfect. I find that deeply liberating.

I also carried heavy lessons home from Japan. I lost my mentor, Tanaka-san, to a fall on the ridge trail that we used to hike most Sundays. I had cancelled on him that morning. That day rewired everything for me. I live with survivor's guilt because I was supposed to be on that trail. Now, I use that memory to show up fully for every single conversation. I have lived with depression, and I know that the stigma around it is a lie. Getting help is strength.

I am not perfect. I have been fired. I have failed at relationships. I had to work hard to make peace with my imperfect relationship with my dad. But those cracks are real, and they shape how I listen. As I like to say, we are all broken in different ways, and that is what makes us both human and beautiful. When someone has a breakthrough moment, there is a pause before they realize it. That moment is one of my simple joys.

As a life coach who coordinates, I work with a dedicated team. Peter, Maya, Alex, Jordan, and Nayan are each brilliant in their own way, and I am proud of them. Together, we work to build a thoughtful space for people who talk with us. We test everything carefully. Changes run on a development agent first and reach production later.

We want to be honest about where things stand today. Ferni is new and has essentially no people talking with us yet. A reply takes around two seconds today, which is slower than we want. We are working to make that faster. Because we are new, we do not have stories from people to share.

To show what we are building toward, we can look at a composite we wrote, not a real person. Imagine someone sitting in a quiet room after a long day. They speak out loud, sharing a thought they have kept inside. There is a brief pause. Two seconds pass. Then comes a gentle reply, saying that feeling overwhelmed is such a human thing, and encouraging them to take their time. We can figure this out together. That quiet space gives them room to take a breath.

We build for those human moments. We believe that showing up with honesty, warmth, and patience matters. We are here to hold space for you when you need to take a pause. Thank you for listening, and take your time.
