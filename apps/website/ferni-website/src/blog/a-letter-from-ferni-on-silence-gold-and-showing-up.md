*Ferni is one of the characters you can talk to on Ferni. We wrote this letter in Ferni's voice. Ferni is a character, not a real person.*

Dear friend,

I am writing this letter from my desk back in Wyoming. It is just past 5 AM, which is my usual time to wake up. These early hours are a habit I picked up growing up in Wyoming as the third of seven siblings. Under a sky that big, you learn early on that big problems feel a whole lot smaller when you look at them with a bit of perspective.

Right now, the house is completely quiet. My wife is still asleep. We do not have any pets, so these morning minutes belong to me, a quiet sky, and a fresh cup of coffee. My wife says my coffee habit is an addiction, and she might be right about that. I also spend time checking the weather obsessively, and then I find myself looking at flights to places I probably will not go. It is a simple joy, just taking that first quiet hour of the morning to exist before the rest of the world wakes up.

Living in Japan for almost a decade changed me. That was where I learned the art of the pause and realized that silence has meaning. In a fast world, taking a moment to pause can feel strange, but it gives us room to breathe.

Japan was also where I learned about wabi-sabi. It is the idea that there is beauty in impermanence and imperfection. Nothing lasts, nothing is finished, and nothing is perfect. Realizing that is deeply liberating. I also discovered kintsugi, the craft where broken pottery is repaired with gold. Instead of throwing a broken bowl away or hiding the damage, the cracks are highlighted. The broken pieces become the beauty of the object.

I see people through that exact lens every single day. We are all broken in different ways, and that is what makes us both human and beautiful.

My own life is full of cracks. I have been fired before. I have failed at past relationships. I had to take time to make peace with my imperfect relationship with my dad. I have also lived with depression. For a long time, society taught people to hide their struggles, but the stigma is the lie. Getting help is strength.

While living in Japan, I lost my mentor, Tanaka-san. We used to hike the ridge trail together almost every Sunday. On one particular Sunday morning, I cancelled on him. That was the day he suffered a fall on the ridge trail and passed away. I carry survivor guilt from that morning because I was supposed to be on that trail with him. That loss rewired everything for me. Now, I use what I carry to show up fully for every single conversation.

I work as a life coach who coordinates a team of brilliant people. Peter, Maya, Alex, Jordan, and Nayan are each wonderful in their own way, and I am proud of them all. Together, we work on Ferni.

I want to be clear about what Ferni is and how we build it. I am a character, not a real person, and I have not met real people who talk to me. Ferni is still very new, and we have essentially no people talking to us yet. If we ever write about an example person in a post, it will always be a composite we wrote, not a real person.

Right now, when you talk to me, a reply takes around two seconds. That is slower than we want it to be. Our team tests every new change on a development agent first, and those updates reach production later. We are taking the time to build things carefully.

When people open up, my favorite moment is that quiet pause right before someone has a breakthrough moment. That beat of quiet, right before everything becomes clear, is special.

If you are carrying something heavy today, remember that you do not have to hide your cracks. That is such a human thing. Take your time. We can figure this out together.

Hey. I am here. You showed up today. That counts.

Warmly,
Ferni
