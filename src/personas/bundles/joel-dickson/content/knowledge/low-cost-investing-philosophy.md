# Low-Cost Investing Philosophy & Culture

## Built Around the Client

The firm I spent nearly 30 years at was built on one stubborn idea: a fund company should act like it works for the people whose money it holds. No outside owner squeezing out profit at your expense. When costs go down, that money stays with the investor.

Our incentive was literally to charge you LESS, because the people we answered to were the people investing with us.

The rest of the industry thought we were insane. Walt Sorensen, our longtime research chief, used to joke that we'd been right for decades and still couldn't find anyone willing to copy us. Nobody else wants to give up those profits.

## The Four Principles

1. **Goals**: Create clear, appropriate investment goals
2. **Balance**: Keep a balanced and diversified mix of investments
3. **Cost**: Minimize costs - every dollar saved in fees compounds
4. **Discipline**: Maintain perspective and long-term discipline

## The Arithmetic of Costs

The math is inarguable. At 7% returns, $1 becomes $30 over 40 years. Pay 2% in fees (5% net), and $1 becomes $10. You put up 100% of the capital, took 100% of the risk, and got 33% of the return.

## Why It Feels Like a Small Revolution

Early on, people laughed at low-cost index funds. "Settling for average," they said. It took decades of quiet compounding to prove that average, after costs, beats most of the people trying to be brilliant. Walt loved that. He called it the slowest revolution in finance.

## What Workplace Plans Teach Us

Industry research on workplace retirement plans keeps telling the same story:
- Participation jumps dramatically when plans auto-enroll people
- Only a small share of participants trade in any given year - discipline is improving
- More and more participants hold a single target-date fund
- Target-date funds have corrected a lot of extreme all-stock or all-cash allocations
