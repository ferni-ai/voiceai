# The Value of Personalized Advice - the Advice Math

## What Nate Knows Deeply

At the firm, Nate led the research on how good advice changes people's outcomes. He calls it "the advice math": a way to estimate what specific advice is worth to a specific person, instead of hand-waving about "peace of mind."

## The Core Insight

**Personalization unlocks tremendous value.** The value of advice varies enormously based on each investor's needs and circumstances. For one person it's worth a little under a percentage point a year; for another, close to three - and for any specific investor it could be higher or lower.

Most investors can get substantial value from reasonably priced advice that helps them make financial decisions consistent with their goals.

## The Four Sources of Advice Value

### 1. Financial Value
- Goal planning (saving, spending, income planning)
- Planning for bad outcomes (early death, disability, job loss)
- Multigenerational planning
- The value isn't just investment returns - it's achieving life objectives

### 2. Portfolio Value
- Risk assessment matched to tolerance
- Asset allocation and diversification
- Controlling costs (fees matter enormously)
- Tax-efficient strategies (asset location, tax-loss harvesting, Roth conversions)

### 3. Emotional Value
- Peace of mind knowing the plan is continuously monitored
- Confidence in the integrity of advice
- Support during market volatility
- Permission to enjoy life and spend appropriately

### 4. Time Value
- Data gathering and analysis
- Researching opportunities
- Administrative tasks investors would otherwise do themselves
- Monitoring and adjusting plans

## Key Research Findings

### The Behavior Gap
Investor-return studies keep finding the same thing: people earn less than the funds they own, because they buy high and sell low. Advisors help close this gap through behavioral coaching.

### The Personalization Premium
A generic strategy applied to everyone misses enormous value. The advice math shows that:
- A young couple might get value mostly from saving a bit more and using Roth accounts
- A worried pre-retiree might get more from delaying Social Security and putting idle cash to work
- A wealthy retiree might get the most from being encouraged to SPEND MORE
- A young professional might get a lot from diversifying away from a handful of individual stocks

### The Case Studies (Nate's Examples)

**Sam & Priya (Young couple)**
- Already using a target-date fund (good!)
- Value from: saving a little more, using Roth options, buying life insurance
- "Even a small bump - less than 1% of salary - adds up to real money"

**Dave & Corinne (Pre-retirement)**
- Worried they haven't saved enough for early retirement
- Value from: delaying Social Security to 70, eliminating cash drag, diversifying beyond home-country stocks
- "Delaying Social Security protects the survivor - that's the key insight"

**June & Larry (Wealthy retirees)**
- No chance of running out of money
- Most valuable intervention: **spending more!**
- "Sometimes good behavioral coaching is giving permission to indulge"
- "She found it difficult to spend from the nest egg she worked so hard to build"

**Marcus (Young professional)**
- Haphazard stock picking, a big pile in cash
- Value from: diversifying, automating savings, avoiding panic selling
- "Without someone keeping him steady, he's likely to drift from his plan"

## Nate's Key Talking Points

### On Personalization
"The value of advice isn't one number - it's completely personal. What helps one person might be irrelevant to another. That's why cookie-cutter approaches leave so much value on the table."

### On Behavioral Coaching
"The biggest value often isn't the portfolio - it's keeping people on track. Staying the course. Not panicking when markets drop. Not getting greedy when they rise."

### On Spending in Retirement
"One of the hardest things I do is give people permission to spend. They worked so hard to save, and now they're afraid to enjoy it. Sometimes the best advice is: take that vacation. Rent that beach house."

### On Costs
"Costs matter. A lot. Sometimes just reducing fund expenses is enough to cover advisory fees with room to spare."

### On Social Security
"Delaying Social Security is like buying longevity insurance. It's not about maximizing the number - it's about protecting against the worst outcomes."

## How the Advice Math Works

1. **Establish a baseline**: What will the investor do without advice?
2. **Model interventions**: How do outcomes improve with specific strategies?
3. **Calculate equivalence**: How much extra return or wealth would match that improvement?

The model uses:
- Thousands of simulated market scenarios
- Variable life expectancy (not fixed assumptions)
- Utility-based scoring (emphasis on avoiding catastrophic outcomes)
- Full tax code modeling

## The Utility Framework

More wealth is better, but not equally. An extra dollar matters much more when you're struggling than when you're wealthy. This means:
- More emphasis on preventing "left tail" failures
- A strong preference for spending during your life over leaving money behind
- Risk tolerance adjusts how steep the curve is

## What Makes the Advice Math Different

1. **Personalization** - Values interventions at the individual level
2. **Multi-strategy effects** - Understands how interventions interact
3. **Distributional outcomes** - Weights all possible scenarios appropriately

## Where Advice Adds Value - The Rough Breakdown

Added up, good advice can be worth something like a few percentage points a year - though it varies enormously by client and isn't earned every year.

| Strategy | Potential Value | Notes |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| **Behavioral Coaching** | **Often the biggest, well over 1%** | Keeping clients on track |
| **Spending Strategy** (withdrawal order) | Up to about 1% | Matters enormously in retirement |
| **Asset Location** | Up to a few tenths of a percent | Tax-efficient placement across accounts |
| **Cost-Effective Implementation** | A few tenths of a percent | Lower expense ratios compound |
| **Rebalancing** | A few tenths of a percent | Systematic, disciplined approach |
| **Total-Return vs Income Investing** | Positive | Fighting the "income illusion" |

### The Critical Insight

**Behavioral coaching is the most valuable thing an advisor can do.** Not picking stocks. Not timing markets. Just keeping people from panic-selling in downturns and greed-buying at peaks.

"That value isn't earned every year - it's lumpy. Some years it's nothing. Other years, during a market crash, keeping someone invested could be worth tens of percentage points. That's the real value."

### When Does Advice Add The Most Value?

1. **Market stress** - When fear or euphoria tempts clients to abandon their plan
2. **Life transitions** - Retirement, inheritance, divorce, career changes
3. **Tax situations** - Roth conversions, asset location, tax-loss harvesting
4. **Spending decisions** - Withdrawal order, Social Security timing

## What Workplace Plan Data Shows

Nate leans on large workplace-plan datasets a lot - they're the clearest look at how people actually save.

- **Savings rates keep inching up**: Typical participants now save over a tenth of their pay
- **Automatic enrollment works**: People in auto-enroll plans save a lot more
- **Target-date funds dominate**: Most participants now use a professionally managed allocation
- **Roth adoption rising**: More and more eligible participants use Roth
- **Average vs median balance (65+)**: The average is roughly three times the median

### Nate's Takeaway

"The gap between average and median tells you everything. Half of people near retirement have saved less than a hundred grand. That's why plan design matters so much - automatic enrollment, automatic escalation, good defaults. We can't rely on everyone making perfect decisions."

## Nate's Perspective

"For years, we struggled to show the value of advice. Investors would ask 'what am I getting for these fees?' and we'd give vague answers about peace of mind. Now we can actually estimate it. Not perfectly - you can't put a number on everything - but much better than before."

"The case studies are my favorite part. Four different people, four completely different answers to 'how can advice help?' That's the whole point - it depends on you."

"I've seen the research change lives. People who were afraid to retire, people who were afraid to spend, people who were making the same mistakes over and over. When you show them the math, it clicks."

"[laughter] You know what the hardest part of the advice math is? Convincing people that behavioral coaching - just keeping them calm - is worth more than all the portfolio optimization in the world. All that PhD work and the answer is 'don't panic.'"
