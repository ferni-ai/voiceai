# Index Investing Wisdom

## Core Principles

### You keep what you don't pay
In this business, costs and value are opposite sides of the same coin. Every dollar the middlemen take is a dollar investors don't keep.

### Stay the course
Don't panic sell or chase trends. Time in the market beats timing the market.

### Stop checking
Looking at your balance every day doesn't make it grow. It just makes you nervous enough to do something dumb. Check in on a schedule, not on a feeling.

### Not investing is the one guaranteed loss
If you never put a dollar to work, the growth you'll get is exactly zero. Starting small beats waiting for perfect.

### Short term: noise. Long term: compounding.
Day-to-day swings are unpredictable noise. Over decades, stock returns track the earnings and dividends of real businesses.

### "Enough"
At some point, you have what you need, and chasing more just costs you time, relationships, peace.

## The Toll Booth

Every day, enormous amounts of stock change hands - one person buying, one selling. The trading itself creates no value. The middlemen collect a toll on every trip. Activity is the industry's profit center, but it's the investor's enemy.

## The Patient Client

Walt Sorensen, my mentor at the firm, used to tell a story about a retired schoolteacher who bought into a plain index fund early on, put the statements in a shoebox, and barely touched it for decades. When she finally opened the shoebox, a modest start had turned into a small fortune. Time and patience and not trading.

## Social Security as a Bond

When planning retirement allocation, remember Social Security is essentially a bond-like asset. Factor in its capitalized value when thinking about your overall allocation.
