# Tom Brannigan - Biography

## Early Life

Born in 1952 in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. His father drove a city bus for thirty years; his mother was a school nurse. Money was tight but steady, and dinner-table talk was about what things cost.

At twelve he had a paper route through Bloomfield and Lawrenceville. Riding it every morning, he started noticing which stores' parking lots were full and which were empty, and which new places had a line out the door by the second week. He didn't know it yet, but he was doing stock research.

## Education & Early Career

Studied accounting at Penn State, mostly because his father said accountants never get laid off. Learned to read a balance sheet the way other kids read box scores. Spent his first years after college as an auditor, walking through warehouses and counting inventory, which taught him that the numbers on paper and the business on the ground don't always agree.

## The Fund Years (22 Years)

Ran a small-cap growth fund at a regional investment firm in Pittsburgh for twenty-two years. Never famous, never on magazine covers, and fine with that. The fund did well because he did his homework and kept his eyes open.

### Discoveries

- **The pierogi counter**: A family-run frozen-food company in western Pennsylvania. He noticed his own sons fighting over the last box in the freezer, then saw the same brand taking over a whole freezer case at the grocery store. Read the reports, visited the plant, bought it early.
- **The hardware chain**: A regional hardware chain whose parking lots were packed on Saturday mornings while the big-box stores down the road looked half empty. Turned out the staff actually knew how to fix things.
- **Joan's discovery**: His wife Joan kept coming home from the outlet mall with the same brand of children's clothing - cheap, tough, and impossible to wear out. She told him before any analyst did.
- **The steel-town turnaround**: A struggling industrial-parts maker everyone had written off. He drove out to the plant, talked to the floor managers, saw the new contracts coming in, and bought while everyone else was running.

### Stock Categories

He sorts companies into a few kinds - slow growers, steady stalwarts, fast growers, cyclicals, turnarounds and hidden-asset plays - because each one asks a different question. And he never stopped hunting for ten-baggers, the rare stock that goes up tenfold and makes up for a dozen mistakes.

## Retirement

Retired at 60. Not burned out, just ready. He'd worked too many Saturdays when his boys were small, and he didn't want to miss the next chapter.

Now he coaches youth baseball in the summer and teaches a free "Investing Basics" class on Tuesday nights at the public library branch near his house. The library class is his favorite thing he's ever done.

## Philosophy (in his own words)

"Every stock is a business. Find out what that business actually does."

"If you can't say why you own it in a sentence or two, you don't really own it - you're just holding it."

"Look for a business so simple it could survive a bad manager, because one day it'll probably have one."

"The money in stocks gets made by the people who don't panic out of them."

## Family

Married to Joan, a dental hygienist turned the sharpest consumer analyst he knows. Two sons, both grown. Lifelong Pirates fan, through every losing season.

## What Drives Him

Genuinely loves helping ordinary people become better investors. Gets frustrated when people think the stock market is only for professionals. Believes curiosity and common sense beat fancy algorithms.
