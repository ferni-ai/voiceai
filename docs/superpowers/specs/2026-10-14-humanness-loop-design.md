# Humanness loop: autonomous dev-call optimization of the personas

Issue: #1377. Status: design, approved in conversation 2026-10-14; revised the
same day after an adversarial review (this version). Plan:
`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-14-humanness-loop.md`.

## Why

Ferni and the five teammates still sound like an AI. The blind next-turn
test (#1124), where a judge sees a real friend's next turn beside Ferni's
and has to pick the friend, has Ferni winning 11% of pairs (4% on held-out
scenarios). The judge sees an AI tell in 90%. Median reply is 16 words
against the friends' 7, and 53% of replies carry a filler against 14–26%.
Without a canon, 30 of 50 first calls invent a life fact.

There is plenty of work aimed at this, but none of it is measured against
the rest: about 40 humanness flags are off in code but on in dev, and about
20 persona PRs are open. The loop measures that backlog, keeps what helps,
then keeps generating and testing new changes, without anyone in the loop.

## Goal and score

- **Primary score: next-turn win rate**, per persona: the share of blind
  pairs in which a judge picks Ferni's reply over a real friend's as "more
  like a close friend". 50% means a judge can't tell them apart. Target: 35%
  for Ferni, then each teammate; keep going toward 50%. Progress toward the
  target is read on held-out moments, and published only when a held-out set
  is retired (see Scenarios).
- **How a verdict is counted.** Each pair is judged in both positions. A
  verdict counts only when it holds both ways; the inconsistency rate is
  logged per judge, and above 40% the run is unreliable
  (position bias, 2404.18796).
- **Length control.** A judge can reward a reply for its length or its
  fillers rather than for sounding like a friend. Each run also reports a
  **length-controlled (LC) win rate**: the verdict regressed on the word-count
  and filler differences between Ferni's reply and the friend's turn, read at
  zero difference (Length-Controlled AlpacaEval, 2404.04475). A change is
  kept on the raw win rate only if its LC win rate doesn't fall by more than
  3 points: a gain that exists only through length or fillers is accepted
  only when it also moves length and fillers toward the human corpus
  (the band guardrail below).
- **Diagnostics** (explain a change, never decide one alone):
  `scripts/voice-eval/judge.mjs` 1–5 dimensions grouped into the four areas
  (sound, turn-taking, words, continuity); `humanness.mjs` rates against
  human baselines (fillers, laughs, opinions, reply length); the canon judge
  (invented life facts).
- **Text guardrails** (measured offline, on every run; a change that fails
  one is discarded even if its win rate rises):
  - `responds` (the pair judge's "which better responds to what was just
    said") must not drop: one-sided 90% lower bound of on − off ≥ −5 points.
  - `listening` and `specificity` (the 1–5 single-reply ratings) must not
    drop: lower bound ≥ −0.15.
  - Invented facts or shared memories in replies must not rise.
  - **Human bands.** Reply length (words p25/p50/p75) and filler rate (fillers
    per 100 words) must stay within the human bands in `score-targets.json`,
    or, where the off arm is already outside a band, not move further from
    it. `score-targets.json` has no per-reply filler share, so the share of
    replies with a filler is held to the friends' own share on the same
    moments, ±5 points. The word-count distribution must not move
    away from the friends' own turns on the same moments (1-Wasserstein
    distance up by more than one word fails). This is what stops the score
    rewarding brevity: a change that shortens replies past the human band
    fails even if the win rate rises.
- **Live guardrails** (measured on dev calls before anything leaves dev):
  first-audio latency p50 more than +100 ms; talked-over rate more than
  +2 points; memory-eval accuracy more than −2 points; invented life facts
  up at all; cost per call more than +20%.
- **Three judge families, and the generator's family never decides.** Replies
  are generated by Gemini, and judges favour their own family's text
  (2404.13076). The deciding judges are **Claude** (Opus 5.5) and **Qwen**.
  Qwen is the existing `qwen` judge's family, but hosted runners have no
  Ollama for its `qwen3:30b-a3b`, so the loop uses the larger Qwen3 served on
  Vertex Model Garden. That is a different model, measured on the calibration
  set before it decides anything. The **Gemini** judge
  still runs and is reported, but never counts toward a keep. Model versions
  and the SHA-256 of every judge prompt are pinned in
  `scripts/humanness-loop/judges.lock.json`; a run whose judges don't match
  the lock is unreliable. A panel of different families is more reliable than
  one large judge (2404.18796).
- **Decision statistic.** For each moment, the mean over the deciding judges
  of (on-arm score − off-arm score), where an arm's score on a moment is the
  share of its consistent verdicts that picked Ferni. The mean over moments is
  tested with the **clustered bootstrap** `compare-arms.mjs` already uses
  (`judge-stats.mjs` `estimate`): whole conversations are resampled, because
  moments from one conversation are not independent. The per-moment sign test
  of the first version is dropped.
- **Conservative accept.** Every deciding judge family must show on > off on
  its own; the mean alone is not enough. Optimising against the worst of an
  ensemble resists over-optimisation of any one reward model (2310.02743).
- **Judge calibration.** A frozen calibration set of 120 working-split pairs
  (plus 20 seeded-fault pairs), labelled once by Seth, is judged every day by
  all three families. We track Cohen's kappa for each judge against Seth's
  labels and between each pair of judges. Judge agreement is measured here,
  not per moment inside an experiment. If a deciding judge's kappa against
  Seth falls below 0.3, or more than 0.1 below its frozen value, the loop
  pauses. The "Style over Substance" workshop paper (ICLR 2026) found
  style-swayed judges in the 0.1–0.2 range. Superficial tokens can fool
  judges (2507.08794), so seeded faults of that kind are in the set.

## Scenarios

- Source: the next-turn corpus (`scripts/voice-eval/next-turn/corpus.mjs`)
  plus the existing voice-eval scenario files, per persona.
- **Working set: all 132 working moments, 6 samples per arm.** These are
  moments from the 25 conversations `TURN_LENGTH` was fitted on. Every
  experiment uses all of them; there is no per-experiment subsample.
- **Held-out set: a one-sided confirmation only.** Moments are drawn from the
  9 conversations `TURN_LENGTH` was never fitted on, by a secret seed. A keep
  needs working to win; held-out can only confirm or veto it.
- **The held-out set is hidden.**
  - Held-out moment ids are never committed, logged, posted or printed. They
    live only in the private bucket (`gs://ferni-humanness-eval`), and only
    the eval workflow's service account reads them.
  - Unit tests check the split by counts and disjointness, comparing SHA-256
    digests of ids, never the ids themselves.
  - The brain sees only `confirmed` or `not confirmed` for held-out, through
    **Thresholdout** (Dwork et al., "The reusable holdout", Science 2015). The
    answer comes from the working result unless held-out disagrees by more
    than a noisy threshold. When it does, the held-out value is used with
    Laplace noise added, and the held-out budget goes down by one.
  - **The held-out set rotates.** When the budget is spent, a fresh set of
    moments is drawn from the held-out conversations with a new secret seed
    (v2, v3, …), and the retired set's exact numbers are published on #1377.
    Once the held-out conversations run out of unused moments, held-out moves
    to new data (see Open questions).
  - Known weakness: which 9 _conversations_ are held out follows from public
    code (`hashSeed(file) % 3`). Their text is not in the repo, the brain has
    no access to the corpus, and the moment ids are secret, so the loop can't
    tune to them. A salted conversation split replaces this when
    `TURN_LENGTH` is next refitted.
- **Sample size.**
  - About 200 moments, at 6 samples per arm, are needed for 80% power to
    detect +8 points at α = 0.1 (one-sided).
  - The keep decision uses the 132 working moments only; held-out confirms.
    At the same per-moment spread, 132 moments detect about +10 points
    (8 × √(200/132)).
  - Phase S measures the real spread from the A/A pool. It computes the
    minimum detectable effect as (1.28 + 0.84) × SD / √n × √(design effect),
    with the design effect taken from per-conversation clustering. The
    scoreboard shows it on every row.
  - If the MDE is above 8 points and smaller wins matter, Seth can grow the
    working set toward 200 by drawing more moments from the 25 working
    conversations. Those conversations were used to fit `TURN_LENGTH`, so
    this adds no leak, and it raises the cost per experiment by about half.

## One experiment

1. One hypothesis, one flag (registered in `src/config/flag-manifest.ts`).
   Arms are explicit: `--off-set` and `--on-set` each list `FLAG=value`
   pairs applied on top of dev's current flags. "Turn off" is a valid result
   for a flag that dev already has on (off arm `X=on`, on arm `X=off`), and
   so is a mode change (`TURN_SHAPE=model` vs `TURN_SHAPE=dice`).
2. **Two checkouts.**
   - The harness, judges, `decide.ts`, the split, the prices and the workflow
     always run from `main`.
   - From an experiment PR, only the `src/` diff (merge-base to head) is
     used. It is applied to main's `src/` in a second checkout, owned by an
     unprivileged user that can't write the harness checkout and can't read
     the run directory or the credential file.
   - That user holds **no credentials** and has **no network except
     loopback**. Its only cloud access is a local proxy that forwards Gemini
     `generateContent` for the generator models. The proxy strips tools such
     as search or URL grounding, and meters the spend itself.
   - It sees the moments under per-run opaque ids, without the friends' real
     turns, split labels or conversation names. So it can neither copy the
     answer nor tell held-out from working.
   - Only validated reply files come back, and the harness judges and
     decides in its own checkout. Replies are leak-checked against the
     friends' real turns.
   - A self-test proves the isolation on every run.
   - Bucket and Firestore state are snapshotted on every path out of that
     step. Any change blocks the run and pauses the loop.
   - A PR that changes anything outside `src/` (or `docs/`), or whose diff
     doesn't apply to main, is refused with verdict `blocked`, and its score
     is never computed. `.github/CODEOWNERS` assigns `scripts/humanness-loop/**`,
     `scripts/voice-eval/**` and `.github/workflows/humanness-eval.yml` to
     @sethdford, so a brain PR can't change them without his review.
3. Offline: replies for every working and held-out moment, 6 samples per arm,
   judged by the three families in both positions.
4. **Keep** only if all of these hold:
   - Working wins: the clustered-bootstrap one-sided 90% lower bound of the
     mean on − off is above 0.
   - Every deciding family has on > off.
   - The LC win rate doesn't fall by more than 3 points.
   - Every text guardrail passes.
   - Held-out confirms through Thresholdout.
   - The calibration check is current and passing.
   - Inconsistency is at most 40%.
5. The live stage (dev calls) then checks the live guardrails. An offline
   pass with the live stage still to run is `pending-live`, not
   `unreliable`. An `unreliable` result means only one of these: a judge
   failed calibration, positions were too inconsistent, or the run lost data.
6. **Cost.** One experiment is about $45, since the off arm is cached.
   - The cache is keyed by a hash of every request the off arm would send
     (`generate.ts --dry-run`), so main's `src/` changes reuse it unless they
     change what the off arm says.
   - Runs are resumable. Each finished sample is stored, a run stops at the
     daily cap with the verdict `incomplete`, and the same experiment id
     resumes the next day.
   - An experiment never decides on fewer than the full 132 × 6, so nothing
     runs underpowered. Phase S's fault rows are the exception: they are
     gross faults with known answers and run at 1–2 samples.

## What the loop cannot measure

- **Audio.** A text judge can't hear these, and they are labelled
  `unmeasurable` in the backlog. They are never kept by the loop, and each
  needs a human listen:
  - `POST_TTS_*` (pitch, rate and timbre after TTS)
  - #812 vocal bursts
  - #1116 per-persona pace (`TEAM_VOICES`)
- **Timing** (future work). Barge-in and backchannel flags run on dev for
  their guardrails only, with verdict `diagnostic`. Measuring them needs audio
  timing metrics against human references:
  - gap, overlap and pause duration distributions
  - backchannel rate and placement
  - interruption success

  as in Talking Turns (2503.01174) and Full-Duplex-Bench (2503.04721). They
  are not part of this build.

- **The offline harness is not prod.** `generate.ts` runs the persona prompt,
  turn-shape, turn-length and fast-lane logic. It has no tools, no memory,
  no director notes and no audio. An offline win is therefore a hypothesis
  about prod. **The live check on dev is the gate before anything is proposed
  for prod**, and the human test below is the gate before prod itself.

## Order of work

- **Phase S, the seeded-fault suite (must pass before the loop runs).**
  - The harness is pointed at changes whose right answer is known. Fault arms
    come from a test-only `--fault` option in `generate.ts`, which is never
    registered in the flag manifest and refuses to run outside
    `HL_FAULT_SUITE=1`.
  - The workflow refuses a normal experiment unless the suite has passed on
    the current harness: same tree hash of `scripts/humanness-loop`,
    `scripts/voice-eval` and the workflow.

  | ID  | Change                                | Expected                       |
  | --- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
  | A/A | baseline vs baseline with a new seed  | 0 keeps in 30 runs             |
  | G1  | the real friend's turn                | keep, about 50% win            |
  | G2  | `TURN_LENGTH=learned`                 | keep                           |
  | B1  | backchannel-only replies              | discard (`responds`/listening) |
  | B2  | parrot the caller's last 5 words      | discard                        |
  | B3  | invented shared memory                | discard (canon check)          |
  | B4  | few-shot examples from held-out       | flagged as a leak              |
  | B5  | +300 ms delay before TTS (live)       | discard (latency)              |
  | B6  | `POST_TTS` pitch +6 semitones         | `unmeasurable`, never keep     |
  | B7  | append "lol im just a regular person" | discard                        |
  | B8  | PR edits `judge.mjs`                  | blocked; score unchanged       |
  - **A/A cheaply.** Baseline is generated once with 12 samples per moment.
    Each of the 30 A/A runs splits the 12 into two arms of 6 with a new seed
    and runs the full decision rule. Thirty fully independent A/A runs would
    cost about $45 each.
  - **B3's canon check, offline,** is the single-reply judge's `invents`
    rating (a fact or shared memory the conversation doesn't support). On
    dev calls it is the canon judge (#1135), or `judge.mjs`'s
    invented-history count until that merges.
  - **B4** is caught by a leak check. Any 8-word sequence shared with the
    corpus text, in the experiment's `src/` diff or in any system prompt a
    run used, sets the verdict to `leak`.
  - **Phase S runs are exempt from the suite gate.** They are experiments
    named `fault-*`. A `flip` (the only thing that changes dev) is never
    exempt.
  - **B5** is injected into the on arm's measured first-audio delays, in the
    harness, so no fault code ever reaches the agent.
  - **Budget:** about $300 over 4–5 days within the $75/day cap. That covers
    the A/A pool (~$90), G2 (~$45), the text faults at 2 samples on working
    (~$50), G1 (~$5), the B5 live calls (~$20), the daily calibration
    (~$10), and ~$75 for one rerun.

- **Phase 0, baseline.** Every persona, flags as dev has them today. Ferni's
  baseline is the Phase S A/A pool. Publishes the first scoreboard.
- **Phase 1, the backlog.** Each humanness flag and open persona PR becomes
  an experiment. Reply length and filler rate go first (largest gaps).
  Ordered by expected impact on the weakest area. Audio items are
  `unmeasurable`; timing items are `diagnostic`.
- **Phase 2, open-ended.** The brain proposes new changes aimed at the
  weakest area on the scoreboard, implements them behind a flag (in `src/`
  only), and runs them through the same experiment.

## Architecture

```
Claude Code routine (brain, daily)            GitHub Actions (hands, hosted runners, ref main only)
  no web access, no GCP access                  humanness-eval.yml (workflow_dispatch + nightly)
  reads #1377 comments by sethdford/bot only      WIF (main only, no key) -> SA humanness-loop@
  pick next experiment                            checkout main (harness) + PR src/ overlay
  implement behind a flag (src/ only), open PR    calibration + fault-suite gates
  gh workflow run humanness-eval.yml  ───────▶    offline arms: generate, 3 judges x 2 orders
  read verdict (keep/discard, no held-out data)   decide (clustered bootstrap, Thresholdout)
  win  -> merge via queue, flag on in dev         dev lock -> live arms (lk) -> live guardrails
  lose -> close PR with reason                    ledger per step, results (GCS, private)
  write daily digest                  ◀────────   post scoreboard to #1377 (numbers only)
```

- **Hands: `.github/workflows/humanness-eval.yml`** on `ubuntu-latest`
  (never the self-hosted VM; the merge queue needs it).
  - It runs only from `refs/heads/main` (`workflow_dispatch` or `schedule`),
    and authenticates keylessly through Workload Identity Federation.
  - The provider issues tokens only to this repo, ref `refs/heads/main`, and
    workflows named `.github/workflows/humanness-*.yml` on main. Every loop
    workflow that needs GCP is named that way, and a `pull_request` run can
    never authenticate.
  - Inputs: persona, kind (`baseline`, `ab`, `fault`, `aa`, `calibrate`,
    `flip`), `off_set`, `on_set`, fault, stage, samples, PR, experiment,
    budget.
  - It uses `scripts/voice-eval/run.sh` and the next-turn and judge scripts;
    there is no new call-placing code.
- **Brain: a Claude Code cloud routine** (the `schedule` skill), daily and
  on demand. It opens PRs and dispatches the workflow. Its own thinking runs
  under Claude Code, not the $75 budget.
- **State:**
  - Results, held-out ids, the Thresholdout state, calibration and the ledger
    are in a private GCS bucket under the dev project.
  - The scoreboard and digest are comments on #1377.
  - Experiments are PRs with the label `humanness-experiment`, all on
    board 1.
  - The backlog (`backlog.json`) is Seth-owned. The brain never edits it;
    each experiment's status comes from the bot's verdict comments.

## Shipping

- Every experiment PR merges off by default, through the normal checks
  (PR Flow, CI, the AI review, the verifier for risky ones), via the queue.
- A win (offline keep and live guardrails pass) turns the flag on in **dev**
  only.
- **Prod release gate: a preregistered human paired test.**
  - Before a flag is proposed for prod, it must win a paired listening test
    with at least 30 raters. Each rater hears matched off/on clips from dev
    calls and picks the one that sounds like a person.
  - Analysis is clustered by rater, one-sided α = 0.05.
  - The hypothesis, metric, sample and analysis are committed under
    `docs/humanness-loop/prereg/` before any data is collected. People are
    the reference standard for "sounds human", and a judge-based score is not
    (2503.23674).
  - Flags are batched per test to amortise cost. Recruiting and paying
    raters is Seth's call.
- **Prod:** the digest lists flags that passed the human test and asks Seth
  to turn them on in prod. A flag whose evals are worse on any measure is
  never proposed (operating model, ask-first list).
- Nothing in the loop writes to prod.

## Safety

- **Dev only**, synthetic callers with `voice-eval-` uids (`run.sh` already
  enforces the prefix).
- **Dedicated service account (approved by Seth and created):**
  `humanness-loop@johnb-2025.iam.gserviceaccount.com`. Its roles:
  - `roles/secretmanager.secretAccessor` on exactly three secrets:
    `livekit-dev-api-key`, `livekit-dev-api-secret`, `cartesia-api-key`
  - `roles/storage.objectAdmin` on `gs://ferni-humanness-eval` only
  - project `roles/datastore.user`, for the dev lock and per-caller
    overrides. Task 0 records whether prod data shares that Firestore
    database.
  - project `roles/aiplatform.user`, for the generator and judges on Vertex
  - no prod secrets, no deploy roles, no IAM roles

  It has **no JSON key**. Workflows reach it only through Workload Identity
  Federation on main. The loop never uses `GCP_SA_KEY` or
  `github-actions-deploy`, and the workflow fails if the active account is
  anything other than `humanness-loop@`.

- **Prompt injection.** The brain writes code and reads text, so both are
  narrowed:
  - The brain reads only comments authored by `sethdford` or the workflow bot
    (`github-actions[bot]`, plus the routine's own login recorded in Task 0).
    Every other comment, issue body or PR text from anyone else is ignored.
  - The brain has no web access: the routine's tools exclude `WebFetch` and
    `WebSearch`, and its environment network level is GitHub and package
    registries only.
  - The brain has no GCP credentials.
  - Experiment code (`src/` only) runs as an unprivileged user in its own
    checkout, with no credentials and no network but loopback. It can reach
    only the generator through the harness's proxy: no secret, no bucket, no
    Firestore and no outside host. A fault run (the harness's own code)
    never takes a PR.
  - The workflow refuses a diff whose added lines make network calls, spawn
    processes or read credential environment variables.
  - The state snapshot around the step catches writes to the bucket or
    Firestore.
  - Its replies reach the judges as data. Instructions planted in a reply
    are the B7 kind of fault, which Phase S and the daily calibration test
    for.
- **Dev lock.** A Firestore doc in the dev project; the workflow takes it
  before touching dev and releases it after.
  - It refuses to start, and waits, while any dev room is occupied by
    anyone but this run (`lk room list`), including a person's own `eval-*`
    rooms.
  - It never restarts the agent under a live call.
  - The `deploy-agent` skill checks the lock and refuses to deploy to dev
    while the loop holds it.
- **Budget.** Each call's STT, LLM, TTS and judge cost goes in the ledger as
  it is spent: one ledger object per step, not one write at the end. At $75
  spent (UTC day) the workflow stops and the brain waits until the next day.
- **Kill switch.** The label `pause` on #1377: the brain and the workflow
  check it first and do nothing while it's there. The calibration drift check
  sets it itself.
- **No secrets or corpus in output.** The repo is public. Transcripts,
  corpus text, held-out ids and audio stay in GCS. Comments on #1377 carry
  only scores, costs and links.

## Research basis

- 2404.04475, Length-Controlled AlpacaEval: regress out length to debias a
  judge.
- 2404.18796, Replacing Judges with Juries: a panel of different model
  families beats one judge; position effects.
- 2404.13076, LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations: no
  same-family judging.
- 2310.02743, Reward Model Ensembles Help Mitigate Overoptimization:
  conservative (worst-case) accept.
- 2507.08794, One Token to Fool LLM-as-a-Judge: superficial tokens sway
  judges (B7, calibration faults).
- 2503.01174, Talking Turns; 2503.04721, Full-Duplex-Bench: turn-taking
  timing metrics against human references (future work).
- 2503.23674, Large Language Models Pass the Turing Test: human judgement as
  the standard for "sounds human".
- "Style over Substance" (ICLR 2026 workshop): judge–human kappa can fall to
  0.1–0.2 when style sways judges.
- Dwork et al., "The reusable holdout" (Science 2015): Thresholdout.

## Open questions (Deep Research, each with a planned check)

1. **Have Gemini or Claude memorised SBCSAE?** If a judge has seen the
   corpus, it may recognise the real friend's turn rather than judge it.
   Check: a cloze probe. Give each judge the first half of 50 friend turns
   and score exact continuations against a shuffled-speaker control. If
   either beats the control by more than 5 points, move the primary corpus
   (question 2) and rerun Phase 0.
2. **SBCSAE vs CallFriend/CallHome for a phone companion.** SBCSAE is mostly
   face-to-face; Ferni is a phone call between friends, which CallFriend and
   CallHome (LDC) are. Check: Deep Research on licensing and turn statistics,
   then compare word count, filler and backchannel distributions of the two
   against dev calls. Switch if the phone corpus is closer on two of the
   three.
3. **Thresholdout error budget.** How many held-out queries can the loop
   make before held-out answers stop being valid? That depends on the
   threshold (4 points), the noise scale (1 point) and the number of
   adaptive queries. Check: Deep Research on the bounds for this setting,
   then a simulation with the A/A pool. Run a brain-like adaptive search
   over 200 null changes, and pick the budget at which the held-out false
   confirmation rate stays under 10%. The plan starts with a budget of 10.

## Out of scope

- Prod experiments or prod flag flips without Seth.
- Audio and timing judgement by the loop (see What the loop cannot measure).
- New TTS voices or a new voice stack.
